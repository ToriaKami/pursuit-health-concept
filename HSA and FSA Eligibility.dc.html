Eligibility

Using your HSA and FSA for more than doctor visits

Yes, you can often use HSA and FSA money for things like a gym membership, supplements, or fitness equipment. The catch is one document: a letter of medical necessity.

With it, spending that is normally out of pocket becomes eligible, and you save roughly 30 percent because it is pre-tax.

How it actually works

What is a letter of medical necessity?

It is a short note from a licensed provider saying a given expense helps treat or prevent a health condition. Once you have one, expenses like a gym membership can be paid with HSA or FSA dollars. It is the difference between “not eligible” and “eligible.”

Can I use my HSA for a gym membership?

With a letter of medical necessity, yes. Without one, a general gym membership usually is not eligible. The letter is what changes the answer, and getting one is easier than most people expect.

What else qualifies?

With the right documentation, HSA and FSA money can go toward supplements, fitness equipment, nutrition programs, massage therapy, and more. The rule of thumb: things that treat or prevent a condition, backed by a provider’s note.

How much does this actually save?

Because HSA and FSA money is pre-tax, you save roughly 30 percent versus paying with regular income. On a $600 a year gym membership, that is around $180 back.
