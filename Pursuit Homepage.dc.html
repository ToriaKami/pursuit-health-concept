Three ways to cover your team, and who each one is wrong for

Nobody sells you the option they do not carry. I carry all three, so this includes the part that is usually left out.

01

A group plan

What most companies have

One plan, chosen by you, for everyone. The simplest thing to explain and the hardest thing to predict. You control the plan but not the price, and a single bad claims year moves your rate.

Right when your team is small, similar, and happy with what they have.

02

A PEO

Admin off your plate

You join a much larger pool of companies, so you get rates a business your size could never negotiate alone. Payroll and compliance usually come along with it.

Right when you want the paperwork gone, on someone else's terms.

03

A health budget your team spends

Known cost

You decide what you can afford per person each month. Your employees each pick their own plan and their own doctor with it. Your cost is set before the year starts and it does not move.

Right when your rate keeps climbing, or your people are spread out.

The formal name for the third one is an ICHRA. That is the only acronym on this page, and your team never has to learn it.

What changes when the number stops moving

Fixed

Your cost is whatever you set. Not whatever the carrier decides in October.

Their doctor

Employees pick a plan their own doctor takes. A group plan cannot do that.

Included

Licensed advisors walk each person through the choice. Not an upsell, not a chatbot.

What your employees will actually experience

They keep their doctor

They choose their own plan, so they can choose one their doctor already takes. That is the part a group plan has never been able to offer.

They are not left to figure it out

Licensed advisors walk each person through the choice and answer their questions, for as long as it takes. Included, not an upsell.

Nobody has to become an expert

Not them, and not you. When someone asks you a question you cannot answer, you have somewhere to send them.

Their money can go further

Through our Truemed partnership, employees can put pre-tax dollars toward the things that keep them out of a waiting room in the first place.

Why I do it this way

I spent fifteen years inside benefits technology, watching the best sales pitch win even when it was the wrong fit for the company buying it.

So I price all three routes and show you the arithmetic. If your current plan is fine, that is the answer, and it costs you nothing to hear it. Every year after that we check again, and if a health budget stops being right I will help you move off it, even though that ends what you pay me.
